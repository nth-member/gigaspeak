Gigaspeak

ASCII carried in colour. Each byte becomes one SPAN whose colours encode its seven bits. The speaker runs the MDQNM engine and transmits its output in a URL; the builder does the same for a message you write; the Communicator turns text into a .HTM file through any of 26 MDQNM transforms. Everything runs in this page.

One press: run mdqnm-timemachines-and-clocks, draw a random four-octet speaker and port, choose at random one of the longest parts of the output that fits the URL, encode it, and open the transmission.

Every grammar is lossless: foreground R, G, B carry bits 6, 5, 4 (00/AA); the background carries bit 3 as its base (00/55) and bits 2, 1, 0 as +AA on R, G, B. The RGBA grammars repeat the byte as the alpha pair of both colours.

The 24 editions

  
The original Gigaspeak ran 24 servers on ports 8790–8813: six grammars × two carriers × two authorities. Here they are the three choices on the Speak tab. The literal authority puts the random A.B.C.D:PORT in the address bar and needs a browser proxied to a local server; run node server.mjs from the repository to enable it, and the JVM engine when one is running.
